Grammarly vs QuillBot vs ProWritingAid: Which is Best in 2026?

May 16, 2026 By Editorial Team 16 min read

Writing assistants have become an essential layer in how most professionals write in 2026. They catch errors that human eyes miss, suggest improvements to style and clarity, and help writers maintain consistency across long documents. The three leading tools in this space — Grammarly, QuillBot, and ProWritingAid — each take a different approach to the problem of making writing better, and the differences matter more than their overlapping feature lists suggest.

Grammarly is the most polished and broadly useful all-around assistant. QuillBot is the strongest choice when paraphrasing and rewriting are your primary needs. ProWritingAid offers the deepest analytical feedback for writers who want to understand and improve their craft. Choosing between them depends on what kind of writing you do, what your specific pain points are, and how much control you want over the revision process.

This review tests all three tools on grammar checking accuracy, paraphrasing quality, style suggestions, plagiarism detection, genre-specific feedback, and integration quality. We also evaluate each tool for different user types — students, bloggers, novelists, and business writers — since a tool that works well for academic essays may be a poor fit for creative fiction or marketing copy.

Key Takeaways

  • Grammarly is the best all-around writing assistant with the most polished experience and broadest platform support (Rating: 9/10)
  • QuillBot is the strongest paraphrasing tool, ideal for rewriting, summarizing, and avoiding plagiarism (Rating: 8/10)
  • ProWritingAid offers the deepest writing analysis with 20+ reports for improving writing craft (Rating: 8.5/10)
  • Students should choose Grammarly or QuillBot; novelists should choose ProWritingAid; bloggers benefit most from Grammarly; business writers should consider Grammarly or ProWritingAid

The Writing Assistant Landscape in 2026

AI-powered writing assistants have evolved significantly from their early days as simple spell-checkers. Modern tools analyze writing across multiple dimensions: grammar and mechanics, clarity and conciseness, tone and engagement, structure and flow, and genre-appropriate conventions. The best tools provide feedback that is not just technically accurate but contextually appropriate — recognizing that a text message, a business proposal, a blog post, and a novel all require different standards of correctness and style.

Grammarly, QuillBot, and ProWritingAid represent the three major approaches to automated writing assistance. Grammarly combines sophisticated grammar checking with tone detection and goal-setting, making it feel like a co-editor who understands what you are trying to achieve. QuillBot centers on paraphrasing technology, offering multiple rewriting modes that range from standard to creative. ProWritingAid provides the most comprehensive writing reports, analyzing everything from sentence length variation to overused words to sticky sentences, making it a favorite among serious writers who want to improve their craft over time.

All three tools offer free tiers that are useful for evaluation, but the truly valuable features — plagiarism detection, genre-specific suggestions, detailed writing reports, and unlimited usage — require paid subscriptions. Understanding what you actually need before you pay is the key to making the right choice.

Grammarly Review

Grammarly remains the most widely used writing assistant in 2026, and its market position is well earned. No other tool matches its combination of accuracy, ease of use, platform coverage, and contextual intelligence. It works everywhere you write — browsers, desktop apps, mobile keyboards, Google Docs, Microsoft Office, and dozens of other platforms — and its suggestions are consistently reliable across different writing contexts.

Features

Grammarly provides real-time corrections and suggestions across four dimensions: correctness (spelling, grammar, punctuation), clarity (conciseness, readability, active voice), engagement (word choice, variety, sentence structure), and delivery (tone detection, formality, politeness). The tone detector is one of the standout features — it analyzes your writing and tells you how it is likely to be perceived, which is invaluable for email, customer communication, and professional writing. Grammarly also includes a plagiarism checker that compares your text against billions of web pages. The Goals feature lets you set the audience, formality, tone, and intent for each document, and Grammarly adjusts its suggestions accordingly. The full desktop app provides a distraction-free writing environment, and the browser extension works across virtually every website with a text field.

Pros

  • Broadest platform support: Grammarly works on more platforms than any competitor, with seamless browser extensions, desktop apps, mobile keyboards, and native integrations with Google Docs, Microsoft Office, Slack, and email clients.
  • Most accurate grammar detection: In our testing, Grammarly caught more genuine errors with fewer false positives than both QuillBot and ProWritingAid across multiple document types.
  • Excellent tone detection: The tone analysis feature is genuinely useful for professional communication, helping writers avoid sounding overly aggressive, passive, or informal in contexts where it matters.

Cons

  • Privacy concerns: Grammarly processes all text through its cloud servers, which may be a concern for users working with sensitive or confidential content. The tool does not offer a fully offline mode.
  • Premium is expensive without annual commitment: The monthly price of $30 for Premium is steep if you do not want to commit to an annual plan.
  • Suggestions can feel generic at times: While Grammarly's suggestions are usually correct, they can sometimes flatten distinctive writing voices in favor of conventional clarity. Creative writers may find the feedback too conservative.

Pricing

  • Grammarly Free: $0. Includes basic grammar and spelling corrections, tone detection, and limited suggestions.
  • Grammarly Premium: $12 per month (billed annually) or $30 month-to-month. Adds full-sentence rewrites, clarity improvements, engagement suggestions, plagiarism detection, genre-specific feedback, and tone adjustments.
  • Grammarly Business: $15 per user per month (billed annually). Adds team analytics, brand tone profiles, centralized billing, and admin controls.
  • Grammarly Enterprise: Custom pricing. Adds SSO, data retention controls, compliance certifications, and dedicated support.

QuillBot Review

QuillBot has carved out a specific and valuable niche in the writing assistant market: paraphrasing and rewriting. While Grammarly and ProWritingAid treat rewriting as a secondary feature, QuillBot makes it the centerpiece of the experience. Its paraphrasing engine is the best available, offering multiple modes that let you control how much the rewritten text diverges from the original. For students, non-native English speakers, and anyone who needs to restructure existing content, QuillBot is the most practical choice.

Features

QuillBot's core feature is the Paraphraser, which offers seven rewriting modes including Standard, Fluency, Formal, Creative, Expand, Shorten, and Custom. Each mode adjusts the degree and type of rewriting, giving you fine-grained control over the output. The Grammar Checker catches errors and integrates with the paraphraser for a two-step fix-and-rewrite workflow. The Summarizer condenses long texts into key points at user-controlled length. The Citation Generator creates citations in APA, MLA, Chicago, and other formats. The Co-Writer combines paraphrasing, summarizing, and citation generation in a single document interface. QuillBot also offers a browser extension, a Microsoft Word add-in, and a plagiarism checker in premium plans.

Pros

  • Best-in-class paraphrasing: QuillBot's paraphrasing engine consistently produces more natural, contextually appropriate rewrites than Grammarly's or ProWritingAid's rewriting features. The multiple modes give you real control over the output.
  • Affordable entry price: At $10 per month (annual billing), QuillBot Premium is the most affordable option among the three tools for users who need the full feature set.
  • Useful for non-native speakers: The combination of grammar checking, paraphrasing, and translation support makes QuillBot particularly valuable for English language learners and non-native writers.

Cons

  • Weaker grammar checking: QuillBot's grammar checker is less accurate and comprehensive than Grammarly's. It catches obvious errors but misses subtler issues that Grammarly would flag.
  • Limited style and tone analysis: QuillBot does not provide the depth of style feedback that ProWritingAid offers or the tone analysis that Grammarly excels at. It is a rewriting tool first and a writing assistant second.
  • Fewer integrations: QuillBot supports browser extensions and a Word add-in, but its integration ecosystem is narrower than Grammarly's. No Google Docs native integration, for example.

Pricing

  • QuillBot Free: $0. Includes 125 words per paraphrase, 1,200 words per summarization, standard and fluency modes, and basic grammar checking.
  • QuillBot Premium: $10 per month (billed annually) or $20 month-to-month. Includes unlimited paraphrasing, all seven modes, plagiarism checking, citation generation, and the co-writer tool.
  • QuillBot Premium + Plagiarism: $15 per month (billed annually). Adds unlimited plagiarism checks with detailed similarity reports.

ProWritingAid Review

ProWritingAid takes a fundamentally different approach from Grammarly and QuillBot. Rather than focusing on quick corrections and rewriting, it provides detailed analytical reports that help writers understand their habits and improve their craft over time. For serious writers — novelists, journalists, academics, and content professionals who write every day — ProWritingAid's depth of analysis is unmatched. It is less about fixing individual errors and more about helping you become a better writer.

Features

ProWritingAid offers over 20 detailed writing reports, including Grammar, Style, Structure, Readability, Overused Words, Clichés, Sentence Length Variation, Sticky Sentences, Diction, Pacing, Alliteration and Repeats, Dialogue Tags, and more. Each report provides a comprehensive analysis of your writing with specific, actionable suggestions. The in-editor integration provides real-time feedback on individual sentences while you write. The Writing Style Guide lets you create custom rules and preferences for your specific project or genre. ProWritingAid also includes a plagiarism checker, a thesaurus, and a document comparison tool. It integrates with Microsoft Word, Google Docs, Scrivener, Final Draft, and most major browsers.

Pros

  • Unmatched analytical depth: ProWritingAid's 20+ reports provide more detailed writing analysis than any competitor. The sentence length variation report alone is worth the subscription for fiction writers who want to improve their pacing.
  • Genre-specific feedback: ProWritingAid offers tailored suggestions for fiction, academic, business, and general writing. The fiction-specific reports (dialogue tags, pacing, clichés) are particularly valuable for novelists.
  • Desktop app for offline use: Unlike Grammarly, ProWritingAid's desktop app can function offline, addressing privacy concerns for writers working with sensitive content.

Cons

  • Overwhelming for casual users: The sheer number of reports and metrics can be intimidating for writers who just want basic grammar checking. ProWritingAid is best for writers who want to invest time in understanding their writing patterns.
  • Less polished real-time experience: ProWritingAid's real-time suggestions are less refined than Grammarly's. The feedback is thorough but can feel less contextual and more mechanical.
  • Limited tone detection: ProWritingAid does not offer Grammarly-style tone detection, which is a meaningful gap for business and professional writers who need to calibrate their communication style.

Pricing

  • ProWritingAid Free: $0. Includes 500 words per check, 10 rephrases per day, and limited reports.
  • ProWritingAid Premium: $12 per month (billed annually) or $24 month-to-month. Includes unlimited word counts, all 20+ reports, rephrasing suggestions, and integration access.
  • ProWritingAid Premium Pro: $16 per month (billed annually). Adds plagiarism detection, 30 rephrases per day, and the full writing style guide.

Best Writing Assistant by User Type

Best for Students

Students benefit most from Grammarly or QuillBot, depending on their primary writing needs. Grammarly Premium is the better choice for writing essays, research papers, and application materials because its grammar accuracy and tone detection help students sound more professional and avoid errors that could affect grades. Grammarly's plagiarism checker is also useful for ensuring proper citation practices. QuillBot is the stronger choice for research-heavy workflows where students need to paraphrase sources, summarize articles, and avoid accidental plagiarism. The Summarizer feature alone can save significant time when reviewing literature. ProWritingAid is a good option for graduate students and PhD candidates writing theses or dissertations, where the in-depth reports help maintain consistency across very long documents.

Best for Bloggers and Content Creators

Bloggers need speed, platform integration, and writing feedback that does not slow down their publishing workflow. Grammarly is the best choice for this group. The browser extension works directly in WordPress, Medium, Substack, and other content management systems, catching errors before publishing without requiring extra steps. The tone detector helps bloggers maintain a consistent voice across posts. ProWritingAid is a strong second choice for long-form bloggers who want to improve their writing quality over time, especially if they publish comprehensive guides or editorial content. QuillBot is less suited to blogging unless paraphrasing existing content is a regular part of the workflow.

Best for Novelists and Creative Writers

ProWritingAid is the clear winner for fiction writers. The genre-specific reports — pacing, dialogue tags, sentence length variation, overused words, clichés, and sticky sentences — provide feedback that is directly relevant to improving fiction. The Scrivener and Final Draft integrations make it easy to incorporate into existing writing workflows. Grammarly is useful for a final proofreading pass but lacks the depth of craft-oriented feedback that novelists need. QuillBot's paraphrasing features are less relevant for creative writing, where preserving your unique voice is essential. Many fiction writers use ProWritingAid for developmental editing and Grammarly for final polishing.

Best for Business Writers

Business writers — people drafting emails, reports, proposals, presentations, and client communications — benefit most from Grammarly's tone detection and clarity suggestions. The goal-setting feature is particularly useful: you can specify your audience, formality level, and intent, and Grammarly tailors its feedback accordingly. The browser extension works across Gmail, Outlook, Slack, and most business communication tools. ProWritingAid is a good choice for business writers producing longer documents like white papers, strategy documents, and annual reports, where the readability and structure reports provide valuable feedback. QuillBot is useful for business writers who frequently need to rewrite or simplify existing content for different audiences.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use Grammarly, QuillBot, and ProWritingAid together?

Yes, and many writers do. A common workflow is to use ProWritingAid for in-depth reports and developmental editing, Grammarly for final proofreading and tone checking, and QuillBot for specific paraphrasing or summarizing tasks. However, running multiple browser extensions simultaneously can slow down your browser, and the overlapping suggestions can be distracting. Most writers find that one primary tool plus one specialist tool covers their needs. For example, ProWritingAid for craft improvement plus Grammarly for final proofreading is a popular combination among serious writers.

Which tool has the best plagiarism detection?

Grammarly and QuillBot both offer plagiarism detection, and they are comparable in accuracy. Both check your text against billions of web pages and academic databases. ProWritingAid also offers plagiarism checking on its Premium Pro plan but the database is less comprehensive than Grammarly's. None of these tools should be relied upon as the sole plagiarism check for academic submissions — institutional tools like Turnitin are more thorough. For general content verification, Grammarly's plagiarism checker is the most reliable of the three.

Are these tools safe for confidential or proprietary content?

Grammarly processes all text through its cloud servers, which means your content is transmitted to and analyzed on external infrastructure. This may be a concern for legal, financial, healthcare, or classified content. Grammarly does offer Business and Enterprise plans with data processing agreements and compliance certifications. ProWritingAid offers a desktop app that can function offline, which addresses privacy concerns more directly. QuillBot also processes text through cloud servers. If data privacy is a hard requirement, ProWritingAid's offline mode is the strongest option. Always review each provider's data handling policy before using with sensitive content.

Which tool is best for improving my overall writing skills over time?

ProWritingAid is the strongest choice for writers who want to understand and improve their craft. The detailed reports — particularly the overused words, sentence length variation, sticky sentences, and style reports — provide insights that help writers identify patterns and habits they can consciously work on. Grammarly's weekly writing insights email is a lighter version of this, showing your trends in correctness, clarity, and engagement over time. QuillBot is less focused on long-term skill improvement and more on providing immediate rewriting assistance.

Do any of these tools offer a free tier that is actually useful?

Yes, all three offer free tiers that are genuinely useful for evaluation. Grammarly's free tier provides basic grammar and spelling correction, which is sufficient for casual users. Approximately 70 percent of Grammarly's value is available on the free plan. QuillBot's free tier includes 125 words per paraphrase and 1,200 words per summarization, which is enough for occasional use. ProWritingAid's free tier limits you to 500 words per check and 10 rephrases per day, which is enough to evaluate the tool but not sufficient for regular use. For daily professional writing, the paid tiers are necessary, but the free tiers are excellent for testing which tool fits your needs before committing.

Which tool has the best Microsoft Word integration?

Grammarly's Word add-in is the most polished, with real-time underlining and suggestions appearing directly in the Word interface. ProWritingAid's Word integration is nearly as good, with the added benefit of being able to run full reports within the document. QuillBot's Word integration is functional but less refined, with fewer features available compared to the web interface. For users who write primarily in Microsoft Word, Grammarly or ProWritingAid are both strong choices, with the decision depending on whether you prioritize real-time corrections (Grammarly) or detailed reports (ProWritingAid).

Conclusion

Grammarly, QuillBot, and ProWritingAid serve different writing needs, and the best choice depends on what kind of writer you are and what you need help with. Grammarly is the best overall writing assistant for most people. Its combination of accuracy, platform coverage, tone detection, and ease of use makes it the safest default choice. If you only buy one writing tool, buy Grammarly.

QuillBot is the specialist choice for paraphrasing and rewriting. If your work involves restructuring existing content, avoiding accidental plagiarism, or improving the fluency of your writing, QuillBot's paraphrasing engine is genuinely best in class. It is also the most affordable option for users who want the full feature set. ProWritingAid is the choice for serious writers who want to improve their craft. If you write fiction, long-form content, or academic work and you want detailed feedback that helps you become a better writer, ProWritingAid's 20+ reports provide value that neither Grammarly nor QuillBot can match.

For most professionals, the smartest approach is to start with Grammarly Premium and then add ProWritingAid or QuillBot if you find yourself wanting deeper analysis or better rewriting support. The investment of $12 to $20 per month in a good writing assistant pays for itself many times over in fewer errors, better communication, and higher-quality writing output.
